# Originality review and research priorities

The `expander_codes` campaign studies the binary adjacency kernel
`ker(A_G over GF(2))`. It stays in this campaign when `--campaign expander_codes`
is supplied. Omitting the campaign rotates the seven graph campaigns. Cached
paper guidance rotates within the selected family.

Completed originality comparisons have three research routes:

* `routine`: avoid another presentation of the same checked result.
* `evidence_gap`: acquire or compare a specific missing theorem. This is not an
  identified mathematical opening.
* `extension_opportunity`: both source-bound provisional assessments cleared a
  stated relative advance. This remains a review candidate, not a confirmed
  original discovery.

Only the last route receives extension priority. At least half the proposal
slots remain available for independent exploration. Source acquisition follows
reviewed topic routes, complete theorem statements and definition/field context;
unavailable papers and omitted context remain visible. Checked library context
prioritizes proof dependencies and relevant conclusions.

The reviewer can represent `target_broader`: removing an assumption from a
published theorem. This requires exact primary quotations, a bounded baseline
guard, matching GF(2) adjacency-code semantics, a sound guard-containment check,
and an independently recomputed finite separating graph. The graph must satisfy
the target and violate the prior extra restriction. Attributing the structured
baseline to the paper remains a semantic comparison reviewed twice; local graph
checks do not certify that attribution or publication priority.

A preliminary possible extension triggers a second tools-disabled assessment
whose job is to find a prior derivation or missing evidence. Each request retains
its trace, usage, duration and source bindings. A failed challenge remains an
error or evidence gap and cannot become an opportunity. Individual request
budgets remain finite; the review envelope accommodates at most two requests.

Before admitting another AI proof request, the coordinator checks exact rational
affine equivalence and sound guard containment against current receipt-validated
certificates. It recognizes rearrangements, positive rescaling, equivalent
integer thresholds and stronger guards. Nonlinear expressions remain opaque;
unresolved implication keeps the normal proof queue. A routine variant keeps its
empirical record and parent binding but does not gain a new certificate. A useful
routine helper can still obtain its own certificate through bounded local Lean
tactics and checked-library reuse, with AI disabled.

The first explicit frontier question asks which multi-cycle structures create
`d-(u+1)>0` and whether a sharp structural bound exists. A retained seven-vertex,
eight-edge graph is replayed locally: binary dimension 1, distance 4, stopping
distance 3. This is a finite boundary witness, not a universal theorem or an
established open problem. Adjacency constructions must be compared separately
from incidence-based cycle codes.

The dashboard distinguishes evidence gaps, candidates cleared by two provisional
comparisons, routine requests suppressed, and certificates added in the current
settings period. Token accounting excludes running requests from completed
denominators and treats missing usage as unknown. Certificate yield and reviewed
relative advances are separate counts. Fixed-target controls and retained pilot
results assess particular behavior; they do not establish a general speedup or
originality. The earlier negative learning, enhancement and literature pilots
remain negative.
